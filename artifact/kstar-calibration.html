Empirical k* calibration against thickness, at a single known Bi content

Ten simulated free-standing GaAs0.80Bi0.20 foils, 2 nm to 1024 nm, solved for k* down all twenty-four available line routes. Because every run is simulated, x is exact ground truth, so k* is obtained algebraically at each thickness rather than fitted. The question the curves answer: which routes are stable enough in thickness to quantify with, and which are eaten by absorption.

The curves split cleanly in two

Every route whose denominator is a hard K line is essentially thickness-independent. Every route whose denominator is a soft L line falls away as the foil thickens — the L photons are absorbed on the way out, and k* absorbs that loss.

Cross-check against the published value

The flattest route reproduces Walther’s Figure 4 to 3%

Figure 4 of the paper quotes k*BiL,AsK and notes it is “almost constant for thicknesses up to 1 µm as both X-ray lines are of very similar energy, so the selection of this line pair would also seem well suited for quantification.” That is the same pair this calibration independently identifies as the flattest of the twenty-four.

This work · Set A mean 2.4161 ± 0.0051 across 2–1024 nm
Walther 2025 · Fig. 4 2.490 ± 0.071
Difference −3.0% 1.04σ — just outside the quoted uncertainty

Arrived at independently on every axis: a different simulation code, a 6.2% higher mass density (5.692 vs 5.36 g cm⁻³ at x = 0.20), and a direct algebraic solve from known x rather than the published k × escape-fraction decomposition. An offset of a few percent is what those differences would predict, and the agreement is quantitative support for the claim that density largely cancels in the calibrate-then-invert round trip. The thinnest-foil value here, 2.4192, lands on the lower 1σ bound of 2.419.

The qualitative claim reproduces too, and extends: 0.7% drift across a 512× change in thickness, and — a test Figure 4 does not run — 0.01% spread across x = 0.01 to 0.20.

On Walther’s axes

Figures 3 (bottom), 4 and 5 plot k* against a measurable K/L intensity ratio rather than against thickness — which, in the paper’s words, “reduces both the dependence on real foil thickness measurements and the effect of detector sensitivities changing with detector type or entrance window thickness … providing an inherent self-calibration.” The three panels below are drawn on the same axes and ranges as his, for direct comparison. Colour is the heavy line; solid and dashed are the K and L denominators. The grey band on each is the constant that figure’s caption quotes.

The offset is a constant, not a trend. It is ~1.5× at 2 nm and ~1.45× at 1024 nm. An absorption-modelling difference would grow with thickness; a fixed scale factor on one line’s intensity would not. Since k* ∝ 1/IBi, our Bi M intensity is ~1.47× lower than the one behind these figures.

The likeliest explanation is already an open question on this project. MC X-Ray’s output carries only Line Ma. If the published “Bi M” is the whole M band, Mα would account for ~68% of it — the right direction, a plausible magnitude for Bi with Mβ as the main companion, and it would produce a constant factor rather than a thickness-dependent one. That is a hypothesis with a number attached, not a confirmation.

Where it bites. A constant scale factor on Bi M cancels in a same-route calibrate-then-invert round trip, so the recovered compositions are likely unaffected. It does not cancel for the sum-peak work: that artefact lands on Bi Mα and its size is specified relative to the Bi M peak, so a “15% sum peak” is a different fraction of our Bi M than of his. This needs settling before any sensitivity level means anything.

The “his” values above are read off the printed figures by eye — call it ±0.1. The three caption constants used in the panels are exact. A 1.4–1.6× effect is far larger than any reading error, so the conclusion does not rest on the eyeballing.

How k* was obtained

Solved directly from Walther’s equations at each run, using that run’s own measured intensities and its known x. No thin-foil-k × escape-fraction decomposition, no fitting.

A is the atomic weight — IUPAC/CIAAW 2024 — with ρ, the atomic density, a separate quantity that does not enter these equations (Walther, personal communication, 10 Aug 2026). That confirmation is what makes the k* values above absolute rather than correct only up to a cancelling factor.

Eq 2
k*Bi,Ga = x · (ABi / AGa) · (IGa / IBi)
Eq 4
k*Bi,As = [x / (1 − x)] · (ABi / AAs) · (IAs / IBi)
Eq 9
k*Ga,As = [1 / (1 − x)] · (AGa / AAs) · (IAs / IGa)

The two line definitions

Principal takes the single strongest line in the shell — Kα1, Lα, Mα. Summed takes every reported sub-line in that shell: more counts, but it pulls in lines that physically overlap other elements. They differ by up to 90%, so this stays an open choice rather than a default.

Bi Lα1 and Lα2 sit 108 eV apart, inside one resolution element, so the reported Lα is already the whole bundle under either definition.

Provenance

Computed by kfactors.calibration_curves from Aggregated\combined_intensities.csv, itself aggregated from the 15 production runs with each .sam re-read and verified against its spec. Intensity column: Intensity Emitted Detected (photons). 133 tests green, golden gate clean.

Nothing here inverts a spectrum back to x. That is the held-out round trip, and it is reported separately.
