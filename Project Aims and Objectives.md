| Project Aim: To develop a Monte-Carlo simulation framework that models X-ray generation, signal attenuation, and detection in GaAs1-xBix thin foils under STEM-EDXS conditions, with the ultimate goal of identifying the optimal X-ray line pairs and geometric parameters for the accurate quantitative analysis of bismuth concentration. |
| --- |
 
| **Project Objectives, to be described as:** Basic Objectives: **LO1: Material Rationale:** Analyse the impact of lattice strain on the GaAs1-xBix compound and the physics of bandgap bowing for optoelectronic value.  The rationale for use for IR optoelectronics is that incorporating heavy Bi atoms reduces the band-gap as well as the temperature dependence of the band-gap. Strain is rather considered an unwanted side effect.  ﻿﻿**LO2: Geometric Constraints:** Use Monte-Carlo simulations for free-standing thin foil specimen geometry for energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDXS) in a scanning transmission electron microscope (STEM) with a fixed 25° take-off angle of the X-ray detector and no substrate to determine the impact on X-ray absorption and escape paths. Advanced Objectives: ﻿﻿**LO3: Signal Attenuation:** Investigate the dependency of X-ray generation and absorption on foil thickness and density (Bi content), specifically focusing on the absorption of bismuth K, L, and M lines relative to either gallium K or L or arsenic K or L as reference  ﻿﻿LO4: Parameter Sweep: Implement a Python-based sweep (200keV, 10e6 electron trajectories), varying thickness across a geometric doubling series of ten values from 2 nm to 1024 nm (2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024 nm) and Bi-concentration (0.01-0.2) to determine the most reliable X-ray line pair for quantification. |
